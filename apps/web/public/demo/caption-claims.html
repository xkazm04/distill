Riverside tram line approved after study finds shorter commutes

The city council voted on Tuesday to build a tram line along the river, the largest transport project in the city for a generation.

Councillors approved the plan by 31 votes to 12 after a debate that lasted almost five hours. Construction is due to start in March 2027 and the first trams should run by the end of 2030.

According to a study by the Harbour City Transport Institute, the line would cut average commutes from the eastern suburbs by 40 percent. The study, published in September, modelled journeys for 85,000 residents along the route.

The project will cost an estimated $1.2 billion, of which the national government has promised to pay about half. The full budget was published on the council's website on Monday.

"This is the most important decision this council has taken in twenty years," said Mayor Helen Ward after the vote.

Opponents argued that the money would be better spent on buses. Traffic on the riverside road has fallen by 8% since 2019 because many offices moved to remote work, the Chamber of Commerce said in a statement.

What happens next

The council will hold public meetings in every affected district before the end of January. Residents can comment on the route until 15 February 2027.

Will the trams be worth the wait? Only time will tell, and the city will be watching.
