Winter road gritting: what the council does and when

From November to March the council grits about forty per cent of the town's roads whenever the forecast says the road surface will fall below one degree. The gritters go out in the evening and again before five in the morning, so that the main routes are safe before people leave for work.

We grit the bus routes first, then the roads to the hospital, the schools and the fire station, and then the steep streets on the east side of the river. Quiet residential roads are not gritted, but every one of them is within a short walk of a grit bin.

Grit bins

There are one hundred and twelve yellow grit bins on corners and hills across Millbrook. Anyone may use the salt in them on the road or the pavement next to it, but not on private drives. If a bin is empty, tell us through the roads page and we will refill it within two working days.

Pavements

When snow lies for more than a day, a team clears the pavements in the town centre and by the station, and the volunteers of the snow warden scheme clear the paths around their own streets with shovels and salt that the council provides.

When it snows heavily

In heavy snow the gritters plough the priority routes in turns, around the clock. Bin collections may be a day late; check the bins page before you put yours out, and leave it at the kerb until it is emptied.

If you see a fallen tree or a blocked drain during a storm, call the council's out-of-hours line. For an emergency, always call 999 first.

Gritting routes

The priority routes are the same every winter. They start at the depot on Station Road, run along the high street to the market square, up Church Hill to the primary school and the health centre, and out along the ring road as far as the industrial estate. A second gritter covers the villages of Lower Millbrook and Ashby, starting with the hill by the old mill.

The map of the routes is printed in the winter issue of the council newsletter and is posted on the noticeboard in the library. If a road on a priority route has not been gritted by seven in the morning, please tell us: it usually means a gritter has broken down, and we send the spare one.

Help your neighbours

In cold weather, please check on older neighbours and people who live alone. The council's community team can deliver shopping and prescriptions when the pavements are icy; call the main number and ask for the winter support line, which runs from eight in the morning to eight at night.

Clearing the snow from the pavement in front of your own house is allowed and welcome. Use salt or sand rather than hot water, which freezes again, and clear a path down the middle first so that people can walk on it while you work.

Parking in snow

When snow is forecast, please park off the road if you can, so that the gritters and the bin lorries can get through. On the steep streets, park in gear and turn your wheels towards the kerb. The council car park on Mill Lane is free overnight when a snow warning is in force.
